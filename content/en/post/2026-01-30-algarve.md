## Taking it easy for once

Having finally freed ourselves, with everything filthy and the longing for a rest winning out, we head for the famous cliff coast.

At Zambujeira do Mar we settle into a well-run campsite: hot showers, a mini market, a restaurant, washing machines. And we take it easy for a while.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/zambujeira-01.jpeg"
  alt="Campsite near Zambujeira do Mar"
  caption="Exactly what we need right now"
>}}

We end up staying almost two weeks. We explore the coast along the Fisherman's Trail to the north and south and find hundreds of photo subjects along the cliffs. What a landscape.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/zambujeira-02.jpeg"
  alt="Cliff coast on the Fisherman's Trail"
  caption="On the Fisherman's Trail"
>}}

{{< figure

  caption="Zambujeira do Mar"

Even the forgotten hobby of geocaching resurfaces here.

## Carrapateira

Time to get moving again. So we pack up and drive first to Carrapateira.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/carrapateira-01.jpeg"
  alt="Beach near Carrapateira"
  caption="Almost a small sand desert by the sea"
>}}

Here we have picked out a nice hike with geocaches - the perfect mixture of exercise, nature and small adventures. The route starts on an enormous beach, almost like a small sand desert right by the sea, before leading up onto the cliffs.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/carrapateira-02.jpeg"
  alt="Cliff coast near Carrapateira"
  caption="And then up onto the cliffs"
>}}

And that surf: loud, wild and absolutely impressive, every time.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/carrapateira-04.jpeg"
  alt="Surf on the Portuguese coast"
  caption="Loud, wild, impressive"

## Sagres - the end of the continent

We have reached the southwesternmost point of Europe: at [Sagres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagres) the continent ends.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/sagres-01.jpeg"
  alt="Coast near Sagres"
  caption="This is where Europe ends"
>}}

The coast here is spectacular and invites some superb walking. Saskia went at it properly straight away: 25 kilometres from the pitch to the west coast and then back along the south coast.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/sagres-02.jpeg"
  alt="Cliffs near Sagres"
  caption="Sagres"
>}}

But then the weather turns. It gets more and more changeable, and eventually a massive storm hits us: gusts over 100 km/h and heavy rain. Mammothon rocks like a boat, and you feel almost seasick.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/sagres-04.jpeg"
  alt="Storm building over the Algarve"
  caption="The weather turns"
>}}

Some campers move their vehicles out from under the trees and park closer to reception.

## Albufeira

Work brings us closer to an airport again - this week I am flying to Berlin on business, and I have picked Faro for it.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/albufeira-01.jpeg"
  alt="Coast near Albufeira"
  caption="Albufeira"
>}}

The pitch we head for seems to be in demand - it is permanently full, so we do not get a space. Right next door another operator has set up, taking in stranded vehicles like ours. On an entirely different quality level, though: the worst since we started travelling. The showers and toilets are effectively unusable.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-01-algarve/albufeira-03.jpeg"
  alt="Cloudy sky over the Algarve"
  caption="And the weather stays difficult"
>}}

Meanwhile the storms over the Iberian peninsula keep building. It does not look like calming down any time soon.
