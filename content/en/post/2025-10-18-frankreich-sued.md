## Brenne nature park

Out of the big-city bustle and into the quiet: after [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris) we need the exact opposite, and we find it in the [Brenne nature park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenne).

Our small campsite sits right next to a tiny but fantastic French restaurant - with genuinely warm people. These are exactly the encounters that make travelling off the usual routes worth it.

## Volcans d'Auvergne

The next target is clear: a pitch near a mountain summit in the [Volcans d'Auvergne nature park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcans_d%27Auvergne_Regional_Natural_Park).

The very first bend on the way up has me thinking: "this could get tricky". Steep, and immediately afterwards a tiny bridge. Shortly after that a local stops us and explains that the road is "narrow". Narrow? Narrow roads come with the territory.

It does turn out to be genuinely steep and tight in places, but we get through. The main car park at the top is full, so we carry on a little further and find a small corner - with a considerably better view.

## l'Aubrac

Further south. First a short stop in Chaudes-Aigues for a work meeting, then on to Nasbinals. While I am still in calls, Saskia takes a long hike through the [Aubrac plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubrac).

The last meeting has barely finished when a WhatsApp arrives: Saskia has found an even nicer pitch. So we pack everything up again and drive a few kilometres further onto the plateau.

## Gorges du Tarn

The next stop is a genuinely spectacular place: the [Gorges du Tarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorges_du_Tarn).

Even the road there is a small adventure - winding, narrow, with breathtaking views and, in places, somewhat nerve-wracking for the passenger.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-10-frankreich-sued/tarn-01.jpeg"

## Millau

Do you know where the highest bridge in Europe stands? Right here - in [Millau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millau). The [Millau Viaduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millau_Viaduct) spans the Tarn, is 2,460 metres long and rises up to 343 metres.

We cross it on our way to a small offroad tour and end up afterwards on a remote plateau - perfectly quiet, surrounded by nature.

Our pitch is again right on the Tarn - though this time with plenty going on: roads closed, runners and spectators everywhere. As it turns out, we happen to be parked at the start of one of the region's biggest trail running festivals.

## Carcassonne

Our final destination in France: [Carcassonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne).

We have played [the board game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne_(board_game)) probably more than a thousand times, so at some point it was obvious: we have to see the real town. And what can we say - it is simply breathtaking.

Next up it is time for a new flag - we are heading over the Pyrenees.
