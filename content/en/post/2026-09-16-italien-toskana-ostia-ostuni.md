## Tuscany: Certaldo

We continue to [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany), and here we stay for a whole week. Near [Certaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certaldo) we find a pitch on a small [winery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winery). The gate leading there is, however... very, very narrow. But Mammothon fits through, and with that nothing stands in the way of our stay.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/certaldo-stellplatz-zypressen.jpeg"

  caption="Mammothon among the cypresses"

And the surroundings are exactly how you imagine Tuscany: gentle hills, [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard), [cypresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_cypress) and in the middle the old town of Certaldo, enthroned on top of its hill. Turns out this is the home of [Giovanni Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Boccaccio), the author of the [Decameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decameron). To be honest, we had not known that, but we are very happy to look around his little town anyway.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/certaldo-alto-skyline.jpeg"
  alt="The brick towers of the old town of Certaldo on a hill between vineyards"
  caption="Certaldo on its hill"
>}}

The old town is a dream in [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick): narrow alleys, towers, a square with a palazzo whose facade is covered in coats of arms, and that warm colour everywhere in the September sun. In between, a large mural hangs on a wall showing collapsed houses and a figure in the foreground. It does not fit the postcard picture at all, which is why we stand in front of it for a while.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/certaldo-gasse.jpeg"
  alt="Cobbled alley between tall brick houses in Certaldo"
  caption="Alley in Certaldo"

{{< figure

And then we find, within walking distance, although "walking distance" here certainly means a few minutes, a [pizzeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzeria) that apparently does not have much to do with tourists. The [pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza), on the other hand? Masterclass.

Saskia even dares to take the [folding bike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding_bicycle) down to Certaldo once to go shopping. The way there is still quite relaxed. The way back to our pitch at the top of the hill is then a rather sweaty fitness programme. Lou watches the whole thing from her seat between the cypresses and keeps her comments to herself.

[Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany) and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) have got us.

## Ostia Antica

After a week we continue to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), or more precisely to [Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_Antica) just outside the city. It is unbelievably hot, but we have a [campsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campsite) with more than 1,000 pitches almost entirely to ourselves. The season is clearly over. The good news: the [swimming pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_pool) and restaurant are still open.

On Sunday, despite the heat, we visit the [ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins) of [Ostia Antica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_Antica). The port city at the mouth of the [Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber) was once the gateway of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) to the sea, and it is genuinely fascinating to see what people in the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) were able to build, organise and use in everyday life around 2,000 years ago.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostia-antica-theater.jpeg"
  alt="The semicircular Roman theatre of Ostia Antica with stone steps under pine trees"
  caption="The theatre of Ostia Antica"
>}}

Besides the [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_ancient_Rome) there is a [Capitolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitolium) with a broad flight of steps and towering brick walls, and in the former [baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermae) [mosaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic) with sea monsters and marine creatures still cover the floor today. Under the [pines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_pinea) between the ruins it is even almost bearable.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostia-antica-capitolium.jpeg"
  alt="Flight of steps and tall brick walls of the Capitolium in Ostia Antica"
  caption="The Capitolium"
>}}

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostia-antica-mosaik.jpeg"
  alt="Black-and-white floor mosaic with marine creatures in a Roman bathhouse, the shadows of the visitors in the foreground"
  caption="Mosaic with sea creatures"

What impresses us most are the public [latrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latrine): a long row of seats next to each other, with a water channel in front. Privacy was apparently overrated. Speaking of which: not everything the Romans invented has caught on.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostia-antica-latrinen.jpeg"
  alt="Roman latrines with a row of seats and a water channel in front of a brick wall"
  caption="The public latrines"
>}}

It is also the first real test for Lou's new backpack. We definitely do not want her to walk in this heat, so she gets to ride on our backs. And it works brilliantly.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostia-lou-rucksack.jpeg"

  caption="Lou rides along"

Right next to the ruins there is also the Castello di Giulio II, a fortress with a round tower and battlements that the later pope [Julius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Julius_II) had built here in the 15th century.

## Dublin: a change of weather

During the week Sven gets a complete change of scenery for work and flies to [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin) in [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland). Quite a contrast, especially when it comes to the weather.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/dublin-pergola.jpeg"
  alt="A gravel path leads through an arbour of vines in a green garden"
  caption="Green in Ireland"
>}}

On the way back, Sven's flight is delayed by more than an hour because of severe weather. And once he is back, Saskia tells him that the storm in Ostia was probably the most intense weather she has ever experienced while camping. So while Sven was escaping the Italian heat in Dublin, Ostia simply decided to deliver some Irish weather of its own.

## Across Italy to Apulia

At the weekend we want to cross [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) lengthwise with one overnight stop, almost all the way south to the [Adriatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Sea). But everything goes so smoothly that we decide to drive the whole way in one go. And so we end up on a large olive farm in [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) with a truly idyllic pitch.

  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/apulien-olivenhof-mammothon.jpeg"

On Sunday we use the time we have gained to hike down to the coast. [Olive trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), sunshine and the [Adriatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Sea): Italy really knows how to do this. For Lou the rock on the shore is a perfect viewpoint, and Sven has her backpack along just in case.

Straight afterwards it is back on the road: on Sunday evening Sven drives to the airport of [Brindisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brindisi), because work is calling in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland).

## Ostuni, the White City

Once Sven is back, we finally explore [Ostuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostuni), the "White City" that towers over our pitch on the hill. And honestly, we had not researched it at all beforehand. So we are completely unprepared for how beautiful the old town is.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostuni-weisse-stadt.jpeg"
  alt="The white houses of Ostuni on a hill in the evening light, a church tower at the top"
  caption="The White City in the evening light"
>}}

Whitewashed houses, narrow little streets, steep steps and an atmosphere that is pretty much the perfect picture of an Italian town. On the square stands the column of Saint Oronzo with a statue at its top, and a little further on the town gets a touch more [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque).

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostuni-sant-oronzo-saeule.jpeg"
  alt="The tall column of Saint Oronzo with a statue of the saint on a square in Ostuni"
  caption="The column of Saint Oronzo"
>}}

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostuni-kathedrale.jpeg"
  alt="The cathedral of Ostuni with a large rose window in the evening light"
  caption="The cathedral of Ostuni"

Next to the [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral), an ornately decorated arch spans the alley, with strollers and restaurant guests streaming through it in the evening.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-09-italien-toskana-ostia-ostuni/ostuni-arco-scoppa.jpeg"

And Lou? She sits down on one of the white steps and looks at us as if asking why we do not just stay here.

Sometimes the places you did not plan for turn out to be the best discoveries.
