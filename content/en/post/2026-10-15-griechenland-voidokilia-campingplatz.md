## Pyrgos Dirou

After reaching the southernmost point, we slowly make our way north again, in several stages. The first stop is [Pyrgos Dirou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrgos_Dirou), where we park right by the sea. After the storms of the past few days, the [coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast) is still pretty wild, and the waves roll foaming onto the rocks.

## Voidokilia

Next up is the famous [Voidokilia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voidokilia_Beach), also known as Ox Belly Bay. While I set up my home office in the Mammothon, Saskia heads out: first up to [Navarino Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarino), then on to the mushroom-shaped bay. Turns out the view is worth every step, and only from above do you see how perfectly round this bay really is.

Overnight stays aren't allowed at Voidokilia, so after work we drive a few kilometres to another beach with a bar. The bar is closed, which means it's wonderfully quiet. Sometimes closed really is better...

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messenien-voidokilia/strand-lou-sonnenuntergang.jpeg"
  alt="Lou stands on the beach, backlit by the setting sun"
  caption="Lou on the beach in the evening"

And we have quite an interesting mix of neighbours: a [Morelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morelo) motorhome that prefers to keep to itself, the owner of a [Tesla Model Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y) who actually lives and works in the car (it doesn't get much more mobile than that), and a young surfer couple who are delighted to discover our open Wi-Fi. Happy to share: everyone deserves a little internet by the sea.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messenien-voidokilia/strand-mammothon-sonnenuntergang.jpeg"

We spend the evening in peace, each of us travelling in our own way. It's another reminder that the people we meet along the way can be just as memorable as the places.

## Back on the Campsite

After quite a while, we're back on a [campsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campsite). When we ordered the spare part last week, we had it delivered there and booked a pitch for the expected delivery window. So we end up with two nights with electricity and showers.

First, the Mammothon gets a proper wash. The paintwork hides a lot, but even by our standards the truck had become seriously dirty!

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messenien-voidokilia/campingplatz-waschanlage.jpeg"
  alt="The Mammothon stands in a covered wash bay, the floor is wet"
  caption="Deep clean for the Mammothon"
>}}

The two days are all about the little things: doing laundry, draining and refilling the water tanks and making the most of the campsite showers. No big adventures, just a few practical jobs and a little extra comfort.

We do get some animal visitors, though. The Mammothon has barely parked when a cat discovers our entry steps, and soon several kittens are sitting in the wheel arch.
