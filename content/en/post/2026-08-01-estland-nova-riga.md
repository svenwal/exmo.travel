## Nõva

After [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) and [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn) we first head back out into the green. Our first stop is on the north coast of [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia) near [Nõva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B5va_Parish). We find a lovely spot in the middle of nature, and a German emigrant has quietly created a little paradise here.

The drive there is partly quite interesting: the roads are generally good, but a few kilometres still lead through large roadworks. Somehow that seems to be part of the road trip too.

With a stock of fresh [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) in the cupboard we continue south to the coast. There a special Baltic experience awaits: the water is so shallow that you can walk several hundred metres out to sea before the water even reaches your chest. After the [fjords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord) and lakes of the past weeks we are still amazed at everything this [Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea) can do.

## Riga

Hardly are a few days of nature behind us when we stumble into the next capital in the next country: hello [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia), hello [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga)! The city centre was warmly recommended to us, and we are not disappointed. Countless small alleys, lots of old, beautiful houses in between and new corners all the time. By the way, [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) celebrates its 825th birthday this year, and you can tell.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-08-estland-riga/riga-825.jpeg"
  alt="Large white letters Riga 825 on a stage, the Freedom Monument behind"
  caption="825 years of Riga"
>}}

If there is anything to complain about at all, it is the city's success: Riga is quite popular, and the centre is correspondingly crowded and touristy. And of course parking Mammothon is a small adventure again. Practically the entire city centre is closed to lorries. But one car park lies exactly on the edge of that area - officially a car park, but just outside the restricted zone. So that is where we stand, within walking distance of everything. You have to find the gaps, especially when travelling at 16 tonnes.

On Town Hall Square stands the magnificent [House of the Blackheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Blackheads), and a few streets further the [Three Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Brothers,_Riga) stand side by side, the oldest residential houses in the city.

[St. Peter's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Church,_Riga) towers over the old town, and right next to it stands an old acquaintance: the Town Musicians of Bremen. [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen) is a sister city of Riga, and the bronze figures were a gift. A little further on, the red [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick) facade of [Old St. Gertrude's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_St._Gertrude_Church,_Riga) appears between the houses.

Anyone who looks closely will also spot cats: the most famous one sits with an arched back on the roof of the [Cat House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_House,_Riga), and according to the legend its rear end points straight at the [merchants' guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guild) that once refused to admit the owner. Very much to our taste. A few streets further a pair of cats stands on a balcony, and whether it is a wedding we cannot say.

Finally, a detour to the [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) district is a must. Riga has so many buildings from that era that you have to look up if you do not want to miss anything. We are at least as thrilled by the [sphinxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphinx), masks and [stucco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stucco) facades as by the cats.
