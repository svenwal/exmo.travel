## The decision

When planning our route back to the upcoming service in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria), we had kept all options open for a long time: straight back through [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) - or the big loop via [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) and the Baltic states? Right here, in [Umeå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ume%C3%A5), it falls: we carry on north and go around the [Gulf of Bothnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Bothnia).

Before we drive even a metre towards Finland, though, there is work to do first. Directly opposite the campsite there is a car park with a stable signal, from which Sven can take his meetings. Saskia and Lou take the hiking trail next door in the meantime.

## Lövånger

After that we move on to a wonderfully remote campsite near [Lövånger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6v%C3%A5nger). The main road is only a few minutes away, but a few kilometres of gravel track make for absolute quiet. We are parked right by the water, looking across the bay at the forests on the far shore.

## Bjuröklubb

The next day pulls us onto another trailhead car park - this time on the [Bjuröklubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjur%C3%B6klubb) peninsula in the municipality of [Skellefteå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5). A lighthouse, a nature reserve and a coastline made largely of bare rock.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-01.jpeg"
  alt="The Bjuröklubb lighthouse with its glass lantern above the yellow keepers' houses"
  caption="The lighthouse of Bjuröklubb"
>}}

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-02.jpeg"

  caption="Red house, blue and yellow flag, sea behind"

The way down to the coast leads over flat, polished rock where only moss, heather and a few wind-bent pines hold on.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-03.jpeg"
  alt="Wide rock slabs with low vegetation and scattered pines, the open sea behind"
  caption="Rock, heather, sea"
>}}

Offshore lie the [skerries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skerry), and in between the rainwater collects in small pools straight on the stone.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-04.jpeg"
  alt="Rocky coast with a small pool in the foreground and a view across the skerries out to sea"
  caption="Looking out across the skerries"
>}}

Particularly beautiful is a narrow rib of rock that lies like a natural dam between two stretches of water.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-05.jpeg"
  alt="Narrow rib of rock lying like a dam between two calm stretches of water"
  caption="Natural dam"
>}}

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-06.jpeg"

Somewhere in between, a stone mermaid sits in the middle of the rock, her tail completely covered in coloured glass. In a spot where there is otherwise only wind and water, that is not what you expect to find.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-schweden-ostkueste/bjuroeklubb-07.jpeg"

{{< figure

The further we explore the Swedish east coast, the more we notice: this side of Sweden has an incredible number of beautiful and surprisingly quiet places too. The west coast gets the postcards - here you get the beach to yourself.
