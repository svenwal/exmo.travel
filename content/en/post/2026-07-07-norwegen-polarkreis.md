## Steinkjer

The decision is made: we are heading further north. But before we take on the next long stages, we treat ourselves to a hike near Steinkjer.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/steinkjer-01.jpeg"
  alt="Walking route near Steinkjer"
  caption="Hiking near Steinkjer"
>}}

The route has been laid out with real care: all along it we find animals and trolls carved from wood. And of course there are plenty of Norwegians out too - evidently a popular local destination.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/steinkjer-02.jpeg"
  alt="Carved wooden figures along the trail"
  caption="Trolls everywhere"
>}}

We are lucky with the weather once again: the day's big downpour catches us exactly when we are back under cover.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/steinkjer-03.jpeg"
  alt="Landscape near Steinkjer"
  caption="Steinkjer"
>}}

## Four suspension bridges

On the way north the next hike is waiting. This time the route takes us across four suspension bridges. Along the way we pass several impressive waterfalls - and the best part: we have almost the entire route to ourselves.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/storforshei-01.jpeg"
  alt="Suspension bridge near Storforshei"
  caption="One of four suspension bridges"
>}}

Only near the end, after crossing the last bridge and with the steepest climb almost behind us, do we finally meet a walking group.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/storforshei-02.jpeg"
  alt="Waterfall near Storforshei"
  caption="Waterfalls along the way"
>}}

And that is exactly where the day's greatest challenge waits: an enormous field of mud. Dry shoes were no longer part of the plan at that point.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/storforshei-03.jpeg"
  alt="Landscape near Storforshei"
  caption="Storforshei"
>}}

## Arctic Circle Center

Today we reach a very particular point on our journey. There was exactly one place in Norway where I absolutely wanted to take a photo: the Arctic Circle Center on the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle). Mission accomplished.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/arctic-circle-center-01.jpeg"
  alt="Arctic Circle Center on the Arctic Circle"
  caption="Arctic Circle Center"
>}}

It is of course considerably more touristy here than at many other places on our trip - but that comes with the territory, and it was definitely worth it.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/arctic-circle-center-02.jpeg"
  alt="Landscape at the Arctic Circle"
  caption="On the Arctic Circle"
>}}

Anyone who has been here before may remember the countless small stone cairns around the centre. Those are now banned and have been removed entirely.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/arctic-circle-center-03.jpeg"
  alt="Surroundings of the Arctic Circle Center"
  caption="The stone cairns are history"
>}}

## Saltstraumen

Sometimes plans change faster than expected. The E6 north is closed because of a massive landslide - we knew that in advance. So we head west first, to the [Saltstraumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltstraumen).

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/saltstraumen-01.jpeg"
  alt="Currents in the Saltstraumen"
  caption="Saltstraumen"
>}}

Here the tides create enormous currents and whirlpools. Impressive to watch - but definitely not a place you want to fall into the water.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/saltstraumen-02.jpeg"
  alt="Whirlpools in the Saltstraumen"
  caption="Not somewhere you want to fall in"
>}}

Then comes the next big decision: the ferry from Bodø to the Lofoten - or the legendary coastal road 17 after all?

  src="/images/blog/2026-07-norwegen-polarkreis/saltstraumen-03.jpeg"
