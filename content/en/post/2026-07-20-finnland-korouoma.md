## Kalix

We follow the [European route E4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E4) north and make one last stop shortly before the border, at a farm near [Kalix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalix). Sadly it is a rather sad sight. You can still tell how much heart and good intention must once have gone into this place - but not much of that is left today.

We even end up as involuntary animal rescuers there: one goat has caught its horns in the netting on the fence and cannot get free on its own. So we help it out. And it was a good thing we did - during our entire stay we do not see a single person.

## Across the border

At the border Sven wants to fill up quickly on the Swedish side to take advantage of the cheaper prices. Apparently a great many Finns had exactly the same idea, though, and had driven across the border especially for it. The queue at the petrol station is correspondingly long - luckily our tank is still full enough.

We spend our first night in [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) in [Posio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posio), already in [Lapland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapland_%28Finland%29). We are parked in an open birch wood by the lake - and discover the glass igloos typical of the region, in which you can watch the polar night and, with a bit of luck, the northern lights from your mattress in winter. In July it is far too bright for that.

## Korouoma

The next day we go hiking in the [Korouoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korouoma), a canyon around thirty kilometres long and up to 130 metres deep, the whole of which is a nature reserve. At the entrance stands a wooden gate in the forest, and after that the trail is essentially: forest.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-finnland-korouoma/korouoma-01.jpeg"
  alt="Large wooden entrance gate reading Korouoma Lapiosalmi above a forest track"
  caption="The entrance to the Korouoma"
>}}

{{< figure

For a long stretch we do not meet a single other person. No hikers, no voices, no cars - only the stream running over the stones down in the valley, and a bridge made of logs laid side by side.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-finnland-korouoma/korouoma-03.jpeg"

{{< figure

Instead we get visitors of another kind: a [reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer) stands in the middle of the track and looks at us for quite a while before deciding that we are not that interesting after all.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-finnland-korouoma/korouoma-05.jpeg"
  alt="Reindeer standing on a sandy forest track and looking into the camera"

And then we find something else all the more often: droppings from a [brown bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_bear). Not once, not twice, but at regular intervals along the trail. Let us put it this way: it definitely made the hike a little more exciting than planned.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-finnland-korouoma/korouoma-06.jpeg"

  caption="Lou takes the shortcut"
>}}

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-finnland-korouoma/korouoma-07.jpeg"

Lou takes all of this considerably more calmly than we do. In the end we do not see a single bear - and we are not entirely sure whether we should be relieved or just a little bit disappointed about that.
