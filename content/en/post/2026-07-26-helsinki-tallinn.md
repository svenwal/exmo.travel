## Helsinki in the rain

On the way south we take a short detour to [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki). We spend one night at the city campsite, and after all the lonely pitches of the past weeks the unfamiliar closeness is almost an experience in itself. Neighbours! Within earshot!

By train we are in the city centre quickly and without fuss. [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), however, greets us with dark clouds and rain on and off. We do not have much time anyway, so we walk straight across the centre and take in whatever can be reached on foot. In between it is always the same routine: [umbrella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella) out, umbrella closed, umbrella out.

On [Senate Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Square,_Helsinki) the white [Helsinki Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Cathedral) towers above its stairs, with the monument to [Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Russia) in front. Lou takes it all with the composure of a dog who has seen worse, and patiently poses in front of the cathedral.

{{< figure

The [Uspenski Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uspenski_Cathedral) looks completely different: red [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick), golden domes, all of it on a rock above the harbour. An [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church) church in the middle of Scandinavia - you do not find that everywhere.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-07-helsinki-tallinn/helsinki-uspenski.jpeg"
  alt="Red brick church with green roofs and golden domes in front of dark clouds"
  caption="Uspenski Cathedral"

And then a small turquoise box suddenly rolls through the crowd: a delivery robot from [Wolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolt), driving its food past the tables as if it were the most natural thing in the world. In [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) the future has apparently already arrived, it just travels a little slower than expected.

## Hello Estonia

The [ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry) takes us to the next country: hello [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia), hello [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn)! Right by the harbour we find a suitable parking spot from which the old town is easy to reach on foot.

We really like [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn). Through the [Viru Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viru_Gate) with its two round towers we walk into the old town, past [city walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_wall) with tiled roofs and through winding alleys.

[Town Hall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hall_Square,_Tallinn) is the heart of the old town, and here it strikes us most: it is pleasantly empty. No crowds, no tourist masses, just a few people with coffee in hand. This is how a city trip is allowed to be.

Something else catches our eye: blue-yellow flags everywhere. World politics is not just something you see in the news - sometimes it meets you in the middle of a beautiful European old town.
