## The Hill of Crosses

After leaving the big city of [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga) behind us, we soon reach the next country: [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania). Our first stop is the [Hill of Crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_of_Crosses) near [Šiauliai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai).

Nobody really knows how exactly this unusual place came about. What is certain is that visitors have put up crosses here again and again over many decades, despite resistance and attempts to ban it in [Soviet times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). And by now there are really, really many of them.

A wooden boardwalk leads right through the sea of crosses. Big and small crosses pile up left and right, [rosaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary) hang from them, and photos, notes and pendants are tucked in between. One cross carries a blue-yellow flag. An impressive and at the same time slightly surreal place.

## Kurtuvėnai

The next day we head to [Kurtuvėnai Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurtuv%C4%97nai). On the rather small car park we actually find exactly one spot left for us - lucky us. From there we start straight on a hike: hills, lakes and above all few people. Wonderful peace after the last few cities.

In the village stands the Church of the Holy Apostle James, and across the fields you can see its towers from far away.

## A pitch with water skiing

After a good meal we are recharged and drive on to a pitch right next to a [water ski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_skiing) track. Completely different views from Mammothon for once: in front of us some do their laps and the pros show impressive tricks, while others are still struggling with the first few metres. Both exciting in their own way, and definitely an unusual backdrop.
