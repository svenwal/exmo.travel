## Olives, olives, olives

Today we cover good distance on the way towards [Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga), driving for hours through olive plantations - what an enormous monoculture. It is apparently the largest growing area in the world, and after a while you believe that without looking it up.

## Álora and the Caminito del Rey

It is the weekend, which means I can take on a hike away from work again. We drive past tomorrow's actual destination and head a little inland to Álora.

The pitch description mentioned "more than 20% gradient". You do not believe everything you read - but those final metres really were very, very steep. And the car park at the top was rather more suited to a small camper van. You still fit.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-andalusien/alora-01.jpeg"

What follows is a beautiful hike around and onto the [Caminito del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caminito_del_Rey) - that walkway which in photos always looks slightly like something you should probably not walk on.

## Málaga - and a longer stop

Now we reach Málaga. Not because we want to see the city, but because I have to fly to Berlin once - dentist - and Mammothon needs a repair to the hot water system, a dripping pressure relief valve.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-andalusien/malaga-01.jpeg"
  alt="Pitch by the dog beach near Málaga"
  caption="Our pitch right by the dog beach"
>}}

Our pitch is right on a dog beach, and the sunsets here are superb. We also run into our tour guides by chance, who are making their preparations here.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-andalusien/malaga-02.jpeg"

Carrying on, we stop "briefly" at MAN, because I find the air conditioning distinctly underwhelming. That actually takes until the end of the following day - at the end of it we have a new compressor.

  src="/images/blog/2025-11-andalusien/malaga-04.jpeg"

And even at MAN there are chance encounters: we are not the only travellers having work done here. By now you simply recognise each other.
