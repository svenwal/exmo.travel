## Melissani Cave

The holiday has officially started! Our daughter has flown in, and a week of family time on [Kefalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalonia) lies ahead. We start with one of the island's main attractions, [Melissani Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissani_Cave). It is only a ten-minute walk from our holiday house, so we could hardly ask for a more convenient excursion.

And we arrive at exactly the right time: The sun shines straight into the [cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) and makes the water glow, and the crowds from the [cruise ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_ship) haven't arrived yet.

## A Day on the Boat

The next day we head out onto the water. In a small boat we putter along the coast of [Kefalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefalonia), past bays with turquoise water where there is no one to be seen for miles. Of course we stop for [snorkelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snorkeling), and Lou keeps an eye on the coast as co-pilot.

## Ithaca: In the Footsteps of Odysseus

Today we head to the neighbouring island of [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca), and this is actually the main reason we came to Kefalonia in the first place: for her birthday, our daughter wished to visit the Palace of [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus). We catch the morning ferry and drive over the mountains almost to the northern tip of the island.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-kefalonia-ithaka/ithaka-faehre-flagge.jpeg"

Whether the archaeological site really was the palace of the legendary Odysseus is still up for debate. The sign says "School of Homer", at any rate. But one thing is certain: it is surprisingly untouched by mass tourism. We have the [ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins) almost to ourselves and can take it all in at our leisure. Lou is delighted.

The ferry won't wait!

## Turtles and Mount Ainos

After that we take it a little easier. That doesn't mean we stop exploring, though. The first bigger trip takes us to [Argostoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argostoli), where we hope to spot [sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggerhead_sea_turtle). And we are not disappointed: watching these beautiful creatures up close is a real highlight.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-kefalonia-ithaka/argostoli-schildkroete-unter-wasser.jpeg"
  alt="Sea turtle swimming just below the water surface"
  caption="A turtle dives down"
>}}

Afterwards we head to Kalamia Beach for a few rounds of snorkelling. The water is lovely, but this side of the island feels a little too busy for our taste. We are definitely more fans of the quieter spots.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-kefalonia-ithaka/kalamia-strand.jpeg"

  caption="Kalamia Beach"

Our second outing takes us high into the mountains, to [Mount Ainos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ainos), the highest peak on Kefalonia. You can drive almost all the way up, but where is the fun in that? We add a few extra metres of elevation by hiking down first and then climbing back up again. The views make it worth the effort!

## By Ferry from Poros

The two weeks on Kefalonia are over, and we head for the [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) of Poros. Everything goes much more smoothly than expected, and we arrive with time to spare. The early-afternoon [ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry) is still sitting in the turquoise-blue harbour.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-kefalonia-ithaka/poros-faehre-hafen.jpeg"
  alt="Yellow ferry at the quay in turquoise water, mountains behind"
  caption="The ferry in the harbour of Poros"
>}}

There is just one problem: it is absolutely packed, and we can't exchange our tickets for that sailing. So we have to wait until 18:15.

We use the time to visit a memorial to a sunken [submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine) and afterwards for some delicious pita at the restaurant next door.
