## Messene

Our first destination for the weekend is the ancient city of [Messene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Messene). First we stop in [Kalamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamata) to stock up on supplies before tackling mountain roads and some rather narrow villages. Driving the Mammothon is an adventure in itself.

When we finally arrive, we find an absolutely fantastic overnight spot: completely alone, right next to the ancient [city walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_wall). It doesn't get much better than this.

On Saturday we go all in and spend hours exploring the ruins. What the Greeks built here almost 2,400 years ago is simply mind-blowing. We visited [Ostia Antica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_Antica) near Rome last month and were already very impressed, but personally I think Messene is even better. What fascinates me most is how the city was built into the hillside: the [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_theatre_(structure)) high above, the [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_(ancient_Greece)) further down, all integrated beautifully into the landscape.

It's hard not to be amazed by what people were able to plan and build so long ago. My verdict: five out of five stars! If you love ancient history, put Messene on your list.

## Vatheia

The next day we head south, almost to the very tip of mainland Greece. We drive down the "middle finger" of the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese), the [Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_Peninsula), to [Vatheia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatheia), a largely abandoned village famous for its striking historic stone towers.

You might think we'd be getting used to narrow Greek roads by now, but today there are two more spots where the buildings leave just enough room to squeeze through. Just enough! Driving the Mammothon through these villages is becoming a sport of its own.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messene-vatheia/vatheia-dorf-berge.jpeg"
  alt="The tower village of Vatheia on a slope in front of bare mountains"
  caption="Vatheia and the mountains of the Mani"
>}}

In Vatheia and all around it there are beautiful stone [tower houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_house). Some are still inhabited, others have been left to the elements. Set against the rugged landscape, they create an almost otherworldly scene.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messene-vatheia/vatheia-turmhaus.jpeg"
  alt="A tall, decaying stone tower among wall remains under a blue sky"
  caption="A tower house left to the elements"
>}}

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messene-vatheia/vatheia-bougainvillea.jpeg"
  alt="Blooming red bougainvillea on a stone wall above a paved path"
  caption="Bougainvillea along the path"
>}}

We park at the viewpoint above the village and explore on foot. The views over the towers and the surrounding countryside are fantastic. Over dinner at a restaurant in the village we notice a familiar pattern: tourists drive up to the viewpoint, take photos of the village… and then of the Mammothon, too! Turns out our truck has become an attraction in its own right.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messene-vatheia/vatheia-katzen.jpeg"

We spend the night here, enjoying the peace and quiet. The next morning Saskia makes the most of the time before my lunch break and hikes up to the church of Panagia Faneromeni.

  src="/images/blog/2026-10-messene-vatheia/vatheia-kapelle.jpeg"

{{< figure

Then it's time to head north again. Vatheia will stay with us as one of those special places where the journey, narrow roads and all, is part of the adventure.
