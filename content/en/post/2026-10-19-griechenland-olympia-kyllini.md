## Olympia

Campsite life is over, we're on our way again. We'd already spotted on the way in that [ancient Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia,_Greece) was very close, so that's today's destination. Amid all the tourist chaos, we manage to park the Mammothon "exactly where we're supposed to": in the bus parking area.

At the entrance, a sign greets us: no dogs allowed! No problem, we think, we have a backpack. So it's back to the Mammothon, Lou goes into the backpack and we return to the ticket office. There we learn that dogs aren't allowed on the site at all, not even in a backpack. So we go in one after the other and each do our own round.

The [Temple of Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Zeus,_Olympia) is truly impressive, just look at those columns! The ancient [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_of_Olympia), on the other hand, is surprisingly plain. Spectators simply sat on the grass back then, no grandstands.

Interesting, sure, but far too touristy for our taste. [Messene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messene) was definitely more impressive. Time to leave the crowds behind and head in the opposite direction!

## Goura

We cross the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) through the mountains, climbing above 1,000 metres in places, until we find the perfect spot near [Goura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goura): wonderfully secluded, in the middle of a field on a high plateau. No crowds, no queues, no restrictions. Just us, the Mammothon and plenty of peace and quiet. Now this is more like it!

## Around Kyllini

The next day is all about off-roading, around [Mount Kyllini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kyllini). The gravel track is narrow in places and pretty badly washed out, winding its way around the summit. The peak reaches 2,374 metres, and although we don't make it all the way up, the track takes us to over 1,700 metres. And guess what? There's still a little [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow) up here, even at this time of year. Not exactly what we expected to find on the Peloponnese.

## A Pitch Among Wild Horses

We've crossed the highest point of our track and are now looking for a place to camp along an even smaller side trail. Naturally, the approach gets even tighter. One section is quite something: first a sharp 270° left-hand turn, then a part of the track that has broken away, followed immediately by a 90° right turn.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2026-10-olympia-kyllini/piste-engstelle.jpeg"

  caption="The track before the tight spot"

What could possibly go wrong? Well... things don't go quite 100% to plan. The video shows that we were close to falling off: the rear right wheel left the gravel road. Good thing the Mammothon stayed up.

{{< youtube BcuTDNqT7f0 >}}

Eventually we make it to the spot we were aiming for, and it's worth it. We're parked all alone, surrounded by sheep, goats and, best of all, wild horses. The view across the mountains and valleys is priceless. No traffic, no noise, no crowds.

Of course, we decide to stay for a few nights and soak up the solitude. Well, almost solitude: a shepherd drops by, and we also meet two other off-roaders who have somehow found the very same remote spot using the same tour guidebook as us. Still, the tricky drive is absolutely worth it. Sometimes the best views come with a few tight turns and a little off-road drama.
