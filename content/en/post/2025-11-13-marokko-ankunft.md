## A flat tyre to warm up

We continue south. There are fewer people and the landscape gets rougher - which is to say, better.

We turn onto the first track, stop, and: pffffff. We have won ourselves a flat. Changing a wheel is on the tour's training programme anyway, we are simply bringing it forward a little.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/errachidia-01.jpeg"
  alt="Landscape in southern Morocco"
  caption="Rougher - and therefore better"
>}}

With the wheel changed, the group heads on to the overnight spot. We instead drive to Errachidia and have the tyre repaired.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/errachidia-02.jpeg"
  alt="View of an abandoned old town in Morocco"
  caption="A view of the abandoned old town"
>}}

Afterwards we are parked with the best possible view of the abandoned old town, among palms and camels.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/errachidia-04.jpeg"
  alt="Palms and camels in Morocco"
  caption="Palms and camels are part of the deal now"
>}}

And then comes the one-more-thing of the day: we visit a family who cook for us superbly.

## Erfoud

Today is the last long stretch before things go offroad.

The first stop along the way is a petrol station. Nine expedition vehicles arriving at once causes moderate to considerable chaos - but at around one euro per litre of diesel, that is easy to live with.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/erfoud-01.jpeg"
  alt="On the way to Erfoud"
  caption="On the way to Erfoud"
>}}

Then we head to the bazaar in [Erfoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erfoud). There is everything here, it bustles, and occasionally as a European you are better off not looking too closely.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/erfoud-02.jpeg"
  alt="Bazaar in Erfoud"
  caption="At the bazaar"
>}}

Loaded with assorted vegetables, very good pickled olives and harissa, we head slightly off the road to camp wild. For the next few days there will be no shops, no petrol stations, nothing.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/erfoud-04.jpeg"
  alt="Wild camping off the road near Erfoud"
  caption="From here it gets empty"
>}}

## Off the road

Now we are away from all roads - from today it is tracks only through Morocco.

We learn our first lesson on tyre pressure and get to apply it on the track straight away.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/er-rissani-01.jpeg"
  alt="Track through the Moroccan desert"
  caption="Tracks from here on"
>}}

Then we stop to practise "driving to within five centimetres": forwards and backwards through a tightly marked course. It sounds harmless. It is not.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/er-rissani-02.jpeg"
  alt="Practice course in the desert"
  caption="Driving to within five centimetres"
>}}

The wind slowly picks up, and the sand starts to move.

{{< figure
  src="/images/blog/2025-11-marokko-ankunft/er-rissani-04.jpeg"
  alt="Dunes in the Moroccan desert"
  caption="Camped in the middle of nowhere"
>}}

We spend the night in the middle of nowhere beside beautiful dunes.
