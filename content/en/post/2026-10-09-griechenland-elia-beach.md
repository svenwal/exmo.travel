## A Noise from the Belt

The drive from the beach near Kastro starts with some background noise that we don't like at all: somewhere around the [belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentine_belt) there is a worrying squeal. The diagnosis is quick, and annoying: the tensioner of our second [alternator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternator) has broken again.

Luckily we had the foresight to pack a spare tensioning rod. So we unload the cabin, tilt it forward and take a look. And then comes the bad news: it's not just the tensioning rod that's broken, a piece of the bracket has simply disappeared as well. Hooray!

That puts the repair on hold until we can order replacement parts from Germany. Our plans for the next leg will probably need a little adjustment. The Mammothon certainly knows how to keep things interesting. As far as I'm concerned, it could do with a little less excitement in the maintenance department.

## Welcome to the Office

Other overlanders told us about Elia Beach: a huge area right on the [beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach) where camping is more or less tolerated. Sounds good! But before we set off, we first have to convince a [snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake) that the [tyre tread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tread) of our Mammothon is not a particularly good hiding place when 16 tonnes start moving.

## The Parts Arrive

On the side I take care of our little technical problem. UPS initially quotes seven to nine days for the spare parts. So we already arrange a campsite for the coming week to which we can have the delivery sent.

But then it turns out differently than expected: on Friday the parts are actually already here! So a quick detour to the campsite, collect the spare parts and back to the Mammothon. At dusk we start the installation.
